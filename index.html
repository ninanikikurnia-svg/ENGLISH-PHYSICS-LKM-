A. GROUP IDENTITY & ROLES
B. EXPERIMENTAL OBJECTIVE

Students are able to prove that the total Mechanical Energy of a marble rolling down a curved track remains constant, and apply the conservation of energy principle to determine the marble's velocity.

C. TOOLS AND MATERIALS

Please check the box to confirm that your group has received all necessary equipment.

D. PROCEDURE & EXPERIMENTAL SCENARIO
  1. Attach one end of the curved track to the table/wall to create a descending slope.
  2. Measure the heights of the following points from the table surface (use g = 10 m/s²):
    • Point A (Peak): Height hA = 0.8 meters (80 cm).
    • Point B (Midpoint): Height hB = 0.3 meters (30 cm).
    • Point C (Base): Height hC = 0 meters.
  3. Hold the marble at Point A (at rest, vA = 0 m/s), and release it so it rolls down through Point B and Point C.
E. DATA COLLECTION AND ANALYSIS

Part 1. Calculate Potential Energy, Kinetic Energy, and Mechanical Energy

Use m = 0.02 kg, g = 10 m/s², EP = mgh, EK = ½mv², EM = EP + EK

Point Height h (m) Velocity v (m/s) EP = mgh (Joule) EK = ½mv² (Joule) EM = EP + EK (Joule)
A (Peak) 0.8 0 0.02 × 10 × 0.8 = 0.16 ½ × 0.02 × 0² = 0 0.16 + 0 = 0.16
B (Mid) 0.3 (Calculate in Part 2)
C (Base) 0 (Calculate in Part 2)

Part 2. Predicting the Velocity of the Marble at Point B and C

According to the Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy, the value of EM at Point B and C is equal to the EM at Point A (0.16 Joule).

1. At Point B (hB = 0.3 m):

EPB = 0.02 × 10 × 0.3 = Joule
EKB = EMA − EPB = 0.16 − = Joule

Using EKB = ½ × m × vB² to find vB:
vB = √(2 × EKB / m) = m/s
2. At Point C (hC = 0 m):

EPC = 0.02 × 10 × 0 = Joule
EKC = EMA − EPC = 0.16 − = Joule

Calculate the velocity vC at the base of the track:
vC = √(2 × EKC / m) = m/s
